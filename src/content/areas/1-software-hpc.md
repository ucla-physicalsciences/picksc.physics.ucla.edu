We believe that kinetic plasma simulation software should be sustainable, reusable and shared. Our group develops and maintains widely used particle-in-cell (PIC) and kinetic codes, including OSIRIS, QuickPIC, QPAD and OSHUN, that run on everything from a laptop to the largest supercomputers. A central part of this work is adapting our codes to new computer architectures, such as GPUs with many layers of memory and parallelism, while keeping them reliable, well-tested production tools. We study how to build PIC software from modular, well-documented components, how to combine different simulation methods (for example, collisional PIC and Vlasov–Fokker–Planck), and how to benchmark codes against one another and against well-diagnosed experiments. We also develop bare-bones "skeleton" codes and educational software, so that students and researchers at every level can understand the inner workings of optimized simulation codes, build their own, and test new ideas.
