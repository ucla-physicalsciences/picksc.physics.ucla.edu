The universe is filled with magnetized plasmas, and with particles accelerated to energies far beyond anything achievable on Earth. Relativistic jets launched by active galactic nuclei and gamma-ray bursts are among the most powerful particle accelerators in the cosmos. We use large-scale, first-principles kinetic simulations to explore the plasma processes that generate and amplify magnetic fields in these environments, and to understand how the most energetic particles in the Universe are accelerated. We study how shearing flows in relativistic jets can generate and amplify magnetic fields, from electron-scale instabilities to large-scale structures, and how magnetic instabilities in jets, such as the kink instability, can convert magnetic energy into energetic particles. Our goal is to understand how efficient these acceleration processes are, what spectrum of accelerated particles they produce, and how both depend on the plasma flow and magnetic field conditions in the jet. We also identify the plasma conditions needed to reproduce these processes in laboratory experiments.
