In plasma-based acceleration, an intense laser pulse or a short, dense particle beam travelling through a plasma pushes electrons aside, leaving behind a plasma wave, or wake. Electrons or positrons riding this wake can gain energy at gradients above 10 GeV/m, hundreds of times higher than in conventional radio-frequency accelerators. When a laser drives the wake, this is called laser wakefield acceleration (LWFA); when a particle beam drives it, plasma wakefield acceleration (PWFA). How the wake forms, how particles are trapped in it, how the six-dimensional phase space of the accelerated beam can be controlled, and how the driver itself evolves are all rich in nonlinear, relativistic and ultrafast physics that blends plasma, beam, accelerator and laser science. We combine theory with full-scale three-dimensional PIC simulations of ongoing and planned experiments, using simulation to test new concepts, guide experiments, and benchmark our codes against well-diagnosed measurements. Our goal is to lay the physics foundations for compact X-ray free-electron lasers and for the accelerator stages of a future linear collider.
